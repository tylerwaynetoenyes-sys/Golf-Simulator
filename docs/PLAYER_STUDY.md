# What golf-sim players want: a short study

October 2026. Desk research, not a survey of our own players. The evidence
is thin: there is one real poll (Opinium, UK), one industry white paper
(National Golf Foundation, 2025), and otherwise reviews, forums and
architects' opinions. Each finding below says how strong its evidence is.

## Findings

### 1. Simulator golf is social first (strong)
- 88% of golfers polled call simulators a good social activity, and the
  same 88% call them a useful practice tool (Opinium).
- Off-course golf (simulators, Topgolf) reached 37.9 million US players in
  2025, more than on-course play for the fourth year running. Two thirds of
  on-course golfers also play off-course (NGF, via Golf Digest and Club +
  Resort Business).
- NGF names social connection as the driver, and says simulators bring in
  people who find a real course intimidating.
- Topgolf Swing Suites, built for groups of up to eight and mixed abilities,
  sell golf alongside party games (venue figures, so promotional).

**For Fairway:** multiplayer, party mode and quick, readable turns matter as
much as realism. Non-golfers need a way in.

### 2. Realistic ball flight is the price of entry (moderate)
- GSPro is the most-praised software among keen players, chiefly for
  realistic physics, at a low price, with a large community course library
  (reviews, Reddit consensus as reported by reviewers).
- "Accuracy matters most" is the recurring forum position.
- A ball that rolls farther than it would in reality is called out as an
  immersion breaker.

**For Fairway:** keep physics honest; never trade it for spectacle.

### 3. Putting is where sims disappoint (moderate, consistent)
- Slow putts track badly, distance and roll feel off, breaks are hard to
  read on a screen, and players fall back on gimmes for putts inside about
  6 feet (forums, Golfers Authority, Garmin users).

**For Fairway:** clear green reading (slope arrows, grid), a configurable
gimme, and a reliable putt mode are worth more than any new course.

### 4. Real, recognizable places sell (moderate)
- The most-recommended sim courses are famous ones, such as Cypress Point
  and the Augusta par 3, played because they are recognizable.
- A badly rendered version of a known course is noticed immediately.

**For Fairway:** our edge is real local places. Each must be recognizable
from the tee to someone who knows it. Fewer, accurate set pieces beat many
generic ones.

### 5. Memorable holes are about choices, not difficulty (opinion, consistent)
- Architects (Hurdzan, DeVries, MacKenzie) agree that engagement beats looks,
  and that holes should be fair but varied.
- Risk/reward holes are the classic example: carry the hazard for a better
  next shot, or play safe. Island greens and forced carries are the iconic
  form; the Sawgrass 17th intimidates more than it punishes.
- A USGA study watched 1,350 shots on forced-carry holes to see who laid up.

**For Fairway:** every course should have some reachable par 5s, drivable
par 4s, carries with a bail-out, and one or two signature water holes. Avoid
unfair blind hazards.

### 6. Immersion comes from coherence (weak evidence, common sense)
- Sounds that match the place (surf, crowds, trees) add to presence;
  mismatched or looping sounds break it.
- Flat textures and washed-out lighting break it too.

**For Fairway:** each course needs its own soundscape (river and towboat
horns, zoo animals, street noise on Delmar) and lively, plausible people.

## Implications for the open list, re-ranked

1. **Putting and green reading** (finding 3). The biggest known gap in all
   sims; check ours first.
2. **Recognizable accuracy** (finding 4):
   - Zoo course: primates at the Primate House, sea lions at Sea Lion Sound,
     River's Edge as it really is, and Forest Park in view.
   - Statue of Liberty: play near the statue, or rethink it as Central Park.
   - Paris: the Seine, its bridges and Parisians.
   - Alton: background streets matched to the real map.
3. **Risk/reward and signature holes** (finding 5): a design pass so each
   course has at least one carry-or-lay-up decision and a memorable water
   hole.
4. **Theme and life** (findings 1 and 6):
   - East St. Louis needs one clear story.
   - Oregon Trail should lean harder into its theme.
   - Cassis needs its vineyard.
   - Every course needs its own ambient sound.
5. **Minimap detail** (contour lines, elevation): it supports green reading
   and strategy, so it rides with 1 and 3.
